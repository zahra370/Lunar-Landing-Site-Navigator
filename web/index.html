Challenge: CLPS Lunar Mission Browser

Where and when should a lander touch down on the Moon?

Pick landing sites and dates. We show which site is best, the best days to land, and what the Sun and Earth look like from the Moon's south pole region.

1. Choose landing sites (up to 5)

South pole map (top view)

You are looking straight down at the Moon's south pole. Rings show latitude and distances are in kilometers. The yellow dot shows the direction of the Sun and the blue dot the direction of Earth at the time chosen on the 3D slider below. Site positions are region centers from NASA LROC tables, not exact landing points.

3D Moon view

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. The bright side faces the Sun. Sun and Earth are not drawn to scale. Use the "Zoom" buttons to fly close to a site.

Sun Earth Recommended site Other sites

Timeline: when is it good to land?

Solid line = Sun elevation, dotted line = Earth elevation. Above the gray dashed line (0 deg) the object is visible. Green zones are the good times for the recommended site: Sun and Earth visible together. The black vertical line follows the 3D slider.

Advanced: sky dome (for engineers)

Center is straight overhead, the edge is the horizon. Each dot is one hour when the object is above the horizon.
